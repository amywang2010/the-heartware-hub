The method

Two microphones, one division.

A stethoscope hears the heart through a filter made of tissue. The method measures that filter from the same recording and divides it out, which is why it needs no second device and no prior scan.

01

Two capsules, one difference

The port capsule sits at the cup. The reference capsule sits behind a sealed cavity on the same board. Room noise reaches both and cancels; the heart reaches one. That difference is 6.0 dB of noise gone before any processing.

02

Estimate the wall

The chest wall is a low-pass filter whose resonance falls as the wall thickens. The recording carries that resonance, so the wall can be estimated from it: 3.7 mm RMS against calipers, no tape measure and no input from the user.

03

Divide it out

The transfer function implied by the estimated wall is inverted and applied to the murmur band. The sound that reaches the app has the body taken out of it, and the correction is one line of arithmetic.

One division, and nothing hidden inside it.

Ĥ(f) = H(f) / T(f, d̂)

H is the recording. T is the transfer function the chest wall implies at the estimated thickness. The hat on d means estimated, and the estimate is the only uncertain term in the line.
