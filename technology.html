Technology

From the chestpiece
to a finding.

Five stages. The physics is the part we can defend hardest, because it is arithmetic: a reviewer can check it, and it cannot encode a training set.

Two MEMS microphones look into the stock acoustic channel.

The port microphone sits at the cup's own 3 mm channel, so the sound path is the manufacturer's rather than one we invented. The reference microphone sits behind a sealed cavity on the same board. Both are TDK ICS-43434: 24-bit, 65 dB SNR, 20 Hz–2 kHz.

That upper limit is deliberate. Murmurs live between 150 and 400 Hz; above 2 kHz there is lung and not much else, and everything added there is noise the correction would have to amplify.

  • Transducers2 × TDK ICS-43434
  • Word length24-bit
  • Bandwidth20 Hz – 2 kHz
  • SNR65 dB
  • Port3 mm, aligned to the cup

The port sees heart plus room. The reference sees room. Their difference is the signal — and the size of that difference is also the quality score.

The room is subtracted, not filtered.

Noise cancellation usually means guessing which part of a signal is noise. With a second microphone behind a sealed cavity there is nothing to guess: that channel contains the room and not the heart, so it can be used as a reference and removed adaptively.

This matters more in the places we are aiming at than in a quiet cardiology department: a fan, a corridor, a shared ward, a home visit with a television on.

  • MethodAdaptive two-channel subtraction
  • ReferenceSealed cavity, same board
  • OutputClean signal + per-frame quality

Bench measurement of the achieved cancellation is scheduled, not yet reported. We will publish the figure with the method when it is measured.

The chest wall is a filter. We fit it, then invert it.

Absorption rises as a power law with frequency and grows with path length; the wall also resonates, and the resonance falls as the wall thickens. Those two terms are enough to describe what the body did to the sound.

The estimator fits that model to the valve closures in the recording — allowing an arbitrary gain, tilt and curvature for the unknown source — and reads the thickness off the residual shape. The operator is never asked how thick the patient's chest is, because that is the one number nobody in the room knows.

  • Trained parameters0
  • InputThe recording
  • InverseRegularised (Wiener)
  • Failure modeRefuses below quality threshold

The demonstration on the Evidence page is this model running in your browser. The Python that produced the figures is in the repository.

What the corrected signal is asked for.

The corrected recording is split into the heart band (20–150 Hz), the murmur band (150–400 Hz) and the lung band (100–2000 Hz). S1 and S2 are located, the systolic interval is measured, and an energy ratio in the murmur band — anchored to the patient's own S1 and S2 — is what a finding is built from.

Because the anchor is the same valve closures used for the correction, a reading cannot be improved by pressing harder, turning up the volume or moving closer.

Where learning stops. The correction is analytic: there is no learned layer to inherit a demographic from. Classification against echocardiography is the next study, and it is the one the pilot is built to run.
  • Heart band20–150 Hz
  • Murmur band150–400 Hz
  • Lung band100–2000 Hz
  • TimingS1, S2, systolic interval
  • OutputSite · band · likelihood · quality

The app is the instrument's face.

Record, look at the finding, keep the examination, export it. The refusal state is not an error dialog: it is a first-class screen, because a confident wrong normal is the failure that matters in a screening tool used by non-specialists.
