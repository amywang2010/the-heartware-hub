Clinical impact

Designed for the diversity
of the human body. Built for
wherever care happens.

A stethoscope is the most widely distributed diagnostic instrument in medicine. It is also the one whose accuracy depends most on the body in front of it.

Different anatomies

The published auscultation figure — 44% of significant valve disease detected in patients over 65 — was found to be useful only below a BMI of 25. Above it, the authors call auscultation a poor screen. That is not a failure of attention. It is a transfer function that nobody was correcting.

Different stages of life

Age thickens and stiffens the wall and lowers its resonance, at the same time as the murmurs worth hearing become quieter and the lungs become harder to clear. A correction that is estimated per recording follows the body as it changes, rather than a fixed filter tuned on young chests.

Different healthcare environments

Rural clinics, community health centres, nursing homes and resource-constrained hospitals share one constraint: the stethoscope is what is in the room. A $59 part with no consumables and no capital equipment fits a budget line that already exists.

Different ways of receiving care

An examination can be recorded, stored and shared, so a frontline worker can send a specialist the sound itself rather than a description of it. The same record supports review between appointments — the part of care where a murmur is usually lost.

Where it is used

First contact, not the echo lab.

The people who benefit first are the ones who examine a chest in ten minutes: primary care clinicians, community health workers, nurses in long-term care, and the patients in front of them.

Three things change. The reading arrives corrected, so the same murmur is comparable across bodies. The decision happens at first contact, rather than spending a referral to find out whether a referral was needed. And the examination becomes a record that can be second-read and trended.

THE ENCOUNTER

Five minutes, once per stethoscope.

Thread the collar, seat the insert, confirm both microphones in the app. The instrument keeps its weight, its stem and its acoustic path, which is the reason a clinician will actually carry it.

THE RECORD

Site, timestamp, quality, waveform.

Stored locally, exported as WAV and JSON. A cardiologist can review the sound, not a summary of it — which is also the only honest way to build a validation dataset.
